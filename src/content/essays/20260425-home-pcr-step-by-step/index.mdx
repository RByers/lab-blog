# Home PCR Step-by-Step

For some people (eg. the immunocompromised or those with other health concerns), regular SARS-CoV-2 molecular testing is an important part of how they stay healthy while still seeing friends and family. Unfortunately, in regions like North America, there are few if any molecular SARS-CoV-2 tests approved by regulators. While approved antigen tests are generally the best option for most people, the addition of molecular testing (even when imperfect) can provide [extra protection](/posts/2024/20240203t220244/) and extra peace of mind. In you are in Europe or Asia, I strongly reccomend the [Pluslife testing system](https://www.pluslife.com/) as more practical and probably more reliable than DIY PCR. In the USA, the only [FDA-approved](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/coronavirus-covid-19-and-medical-devices/home-otc-covid-19-diagnostic-tests#list) home molecular SARS-CoV-2 tests are [Metrix](https://shop.aptitudemedical.com/products/metrix-covid-19-test) and [3eo](https://verseadiagnostics.com/products/3eo-health-covid-19-starter-pack-1), but I have used neither. If, like me, you are in Canada, you are currently completely out of luck for Health Canada approved molecular testing. The companies behind prior good quality products like Lucira Check-it and Cue have gone out of business, due, I believe, to the failure of the regulatory bodies to have pragmatic and predictable approval programs.

This guide is intended for those who want to try getting into experimenting with do-it-yourself PCR for viruses like SARS-CoV-2. The instructions and products listed here are for research and education purposes only due to the liklihood of error and possibility of false negatives. Where testing is important for your health, you should rely on tests approved by your local regulatory bodies. But if you are curious and can afford to spend a few thousand dollars just for interests sake, then read on!

## Objectives

For any experimental setup it's important to first be clear on what exactly you're trying to achieve. There are always tradeoffs, so it's useful to be clear on how you are balancing priorities. My goals here are:
 - Detect SARS-CoV-2 RNA in a nasal swab sample
 - Using molecular biology laboratory techniques, not consumer devices
 - Lowest possible upfront and per-test costs
 - Minimize time and education required, though simplicity is a non-goal (use consumer tests for that)
 - Some mechanism for differentiating strong vs. weak positives, though precise quantification is out-of-scope
 - Reliable enough to be a useful supplement to antigen testing, not a replacement for it

## Lab setup

To get started you'll need a bunch of molecular biology equipment, most critically a PCR machine. I reccomend the [miniPCR mini8x](https://www.minipcr.com/product/minipcr-mini8x-thermal-cycler/), though the [Bento Lab](https://bento.bio/) also looks interesting. Low cost equipment can be readily ordered from educational suppliers like [miniPCR](https://www.minipcr.com), and generic lab supplies can be ordered from [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/). Some of the reagents required can only be ordered from lab supply companies which ship only to businesses and legitimate research organizations, presumably for biosafety reasons.
